Exact here because μ is constant inside each uniform region and the beam has one photon energy. Real anatomy varies continuously (approximated by small voxel steps) and real tubes add beam hardening, scatter, finite beam width, and noise.

How does the scanner get this number?

A detector counts the photons that arrive, Nd. The scanner also knows N₀, the count with nothing in the beam. For each ray it stores g = −ln(Nd/N₀). With a beam of one energy, that equals the sum of μ × L along the ray. For this ray, Nd/N₀ = e−g = 0.135, so g = 2.000.

A source and a detector face each other across the object. They slide sideways to set the ray’s position ℓ, then the pair turns to a new angle θ. At one angle every ray is parallel.
