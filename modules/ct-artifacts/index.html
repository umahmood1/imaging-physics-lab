CT Artifacts: What You See, the Cause, and the Fix

An artifact is something in the image that is not in the object scanned. Each case below shows one artifact in a phantom (a head, a chest, or an abdomen), beside the fault in the scanner’s data that causes it. The cases are grouped by what the artifact looks like, as the textbooks group them: streaks, and rings and bands. Streaks have many causes, so each cause has its own case. For each you get what a reader sees, the cause, the physics that gives it its shape, and the fix. The scanner these faults come from →

The physics: why it has this shape

Δμ(x,y)=one view’s shareNπT​​​k∑​error of reading kεk​​​c~(xcosβk​+ysinβk​−ℓk​)

This is the reconstruction sum (Prince and Links, Eq. 6.61, written for the N = 984 views of a full turn), applied to the errors alone. Reconstruction filters every reading with c̃ and lays it along its own ray, the line x cos βk + y sin βk = ℓk, because nothing in a reading says where along the ray the attenuation was. c̃ is positive on the ray and negative just beside it, so a reading that is wrong by εk adds a bright line with dark edges, or the reverse. In a fault-free scan each line is cancelled by its neighbors and by the other views. An error has no partner, so it stays: that is a streak. All the rays of one view leave the same focal spot, so the streaks of a spoiled view meet at the tube. T is the detector spacing, 0.56 mm. Each εk is noise of about one over the square root of the photons counted, so the rays through the most tissue make the strongest streaks. With the tube at 0.1% of its output, the ray through the most tissue counts about 11 photons where a normal view counts about 10,800.

What you see
A fan of thin straight streaks, bright and dark side by side. Followed back, they meet at one point outside the phantom: where the X-ray tube was.
Cause
The X-ray tube, not a detector. A short circuit inside the tube (an arc) makes the power supply switch it off for a few milliseconds. For one view, or a few, almost no X-rays reach the phantom, and every reading of that view is mostly noise.
The fix
The power supply detects the arc. The scanner discards the spoiled view and rebuilds it from the views on either side (try it under “More controls”). Frequent arcing means the tube is near the end of its life.
