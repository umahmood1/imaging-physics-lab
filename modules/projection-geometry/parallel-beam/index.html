Parallel beam: how much of the beam lands on each part of a detector?

A uniform parallel beam lands on a detector split into two regions. To find what each region receives, find the area of the beam inside region 1 and inside region 2, then multiply each area by the intensity. Prince & Links Problems 5.22 and 5.24 both depend on this split, and the Book problems step works through each. The Explore tab has the point-source version.

What are we finding?

An X-ray beam lands on a detector. The detector has two separate patches, region 1 and region 2. Each patch adds up all the X-ray power that lands on it. We want the power each patch receives: P₁ and P₂.

I0​=10.0 mW/cm2beam: 6.0×8.0 cm
ƒ Show the algebra

What intensity means. Intensity is power per unit area: I=AP​. The unit mW/cm² reads “milliwatts on each square centimeter”.

So I₀ = 10.0 mW/cm² means every 1 cm × 1 cm square inside the beam gets 10.0 mW. A uniform beam has the same value everywhere inside it, and zero outside.
