Projection Geometry and Detector Signal

Trace rays from a point source through selectable attenuating objects and a collimated field to a flat detector. See how distance, angle, and the material in the way set the signal at each point of the detector. The Parallel beam tab does the same for a beam of parallel rays. The model uses one X-ray energy.

1. Set the coordinates

The source is at (z, xd, yd) = (0, 0, 0). The central ray travels in +z to the flat detector, which sits at z = SID, the source-to-image distance. On that detector, xd runs horizontally and yd runs upward. The ray-path drawing is a z–yd projection.

The object sits between source and detector at depth SOD, the source-to-object distance. The object is also limited from side to side, so rays aimed far enough to the left or right miss it. The dark blades near the source are the collimator. They limit the beam to an opening.

Quick check

Does the central ray have a nonzero detector xd or yd coordinate?
