About this site

About the Medical Imaging Physics Lab

The Medical Imaging Physics Lab is a set of interactive simulations for teaching the physics of X-ray imaging and computed tomography (CT) to graduate students. Every picture and number is computed in your browser from a physics model, so you can change a setting and see what the physics does. There is nothing to install and no account to create.

Why it was built

The site was built for a graduate course in X-ray imaging and computed tomography and is revised after each round of feedback from students. It is used in lectures and by students working on their own.

What the models rest on

  • Checked against published sources. Image noise on the CT scanner page is calibrated to daily quality-control scans of clinical scanners (Bache et al., J Appl Clin Med Phys 2017;18(2):163–169). Attenuation coefficients are from NIST tables. The chest X-ray AI model’s scores agree with its Python library (TorchXRayVision) to the three decimals shown, on all 14 example radiographs. Artifact shapes are compared with Hsieh, Computed Tomography: Principles, Design, Artifacts, and Recent Advances, 3rd ed.
  • Representative values. Some scanner dimensions, focal spot sizes, and the reconstruction kernel’s resolution are typical of a 64-slice scanner. They have not been checked against a manufacturer’s technical reference manual.
  • Not modeled. Helical scanning, tube-current modulation, iterative reconstruction, and dose in mGy.
  • Textbooks. The pages follow Prince and Links, Medical Imaging Signals and Systems, 2nd ed. All explanations and figures are original, and every value is computed by the site.

Limits

These are teaching models. They are not for clinical use, and no number here is a measurement of a patient or of a particular scanner. Chest radiographs in the image-comparison activity are from the NIH ChestX-ray14 dataset (NIH Clinical Center). That activity’s AI model (TorchXRayVision DenseNet121, NIH weights) runs in your browser, and the images are not sent anywhere.

The site is independent instructional work. It is not an official publication of any institution.
